## Post-close strategy change — §1.3 leg confirmation rewritten
Prompted by the question "does the 15-minute gate actually make sense?" The
review said mostly no, and the rule changed as a result.

**What the record showed.**
1. The clock was never independent of the volume clause. On 5-min bars,
   "several consecutive closes" *is* 15 minutes — three bars, by construction.
   One measurement was being stated twice and counted as two tests.
2. The founding cases (7/21, `4654c05`) turned on volume, never duration: NVS
   was declined at ~20 min of sustain on *declining* volume and accepted at
   ~15 min on *rising* volume; TSM was declined after a 90-minute grind with no
   volume surge and accepted at ~20 min with one. Duration was satisfied in
   both declines. The 15-minute figure looks back-derived from the bar count.
3. In four of the five live declines the volume test failed independently —
   SMCI 0.9×, BIRK-1st collapsing, RDDT-1st (three simultaneous fails),
   RDDT-2nd 0.42×. The clock was redundant in all four.
4. The one case where it bound alone — BIRK's second leg on 8/13, "holding
   strictly on the clock, not the setup quality — 14 min, need 15" — was
   decided by 60 seconds. It failed at ~16 min and the decline was vindicated,
   but one minute earlier and we buy and lose. That is a coin landing well.

Also noted: the rule needed a patch saying a rolling-over leg "does not qualify
by aging into the window," which concedes that time-in-force means nothing
without a health check — and if the health check decides, the clock is not
load-bearing.

**Changes made.**
- **Veto now rests on volume + structure.** `late_entry_min_volume_ratio: 1.5`
  (codifies existing discretion — consistent with every live call on record:
  passed NBIS 2.9× / BIRK 3.35×, rejected RDDT 1.23%, SMCI 0.9×, RDDT 0.42×)
  and `late_entry_require_structure: true` (a higher low or new extreme, made
  AND held; breaking the sequence low is an immediate disqualification — the
  thing that actually killed RDDT at 10:26 and BIRK at 10:42).
- **Leg age demoted to advisory** (`late_entry_advisory_leg_minutes: 15`),
  recorded but never blocking. Not disproven, only unproven: the gap-supply
  mechanism is plausible, so the figure stays measured.
- **`data/leg_log.csv` added** — every evaluated leg, accepted *and* declined,
  with age, volume ratio, structure, blocking reason, outcome. Backfilled with
  the 12 cases reconstructable from the journals. The bias in the old evidence
  was worse than stated above: the sample was *generated by the rule*, since
  legs only get measured when they are "almost qualified," which is exactly
  when a dying leg is most conspicuous. Nothing here gets tightened until this
  file contains acceptances too.
- **Cadence justification softened** in entry.md / STRATEGY.md / README. The
  5-min re-check was defended as "a faster cadence cannot detect anything a
  5-minute one would miss," which leaned on the strong form of the clock rule.
  BIRK is the counterexample: it qualified at one sample and was dead at the
  next. The 5-min cadence stands on the bar length instead — a sub-5-min loop
  re-reads the same bar — with the honest note that this is not a proof.

## Post-close review — the liquidity constraint
Reviewed on the standing belief that liquidity gates, not tape rules, were the
binding constraint blocking good trades. **The two flagship cases both resolve
against that belief.**

- **NBIS 8/12** (blocked 12:27 on quote-size: bid 5/ask 8 vs. a flat 10, with a
  perfect tape — +27.4%, 2.9x volume, spread 2.08%). Four minutes later the same
  $250C strike showed **bid 32/ask 93**, OI unchanged. It was taken at 12:33 and
  realized **-$40 (-1.27%)**. The gate cost six minutes.
- **SE 8/11** (54 checks, spread and size never simultaneously clean). It was
  paper-traded through the gate-exception shadow and **stopped out at -25.9%
  (-$360)**. The gate saved that.
- Backdrop: trades that clear every gate have realized **-$3,012 across 12
  trading days** (4 win days, 8 loss days). No evidence the gates are expensive.
  Not evidence they select *well* either — the blocked set is not a random
  sample — but the burden of proof sits with loosening.

**The one real bug: the step-out searched the wrong axis.** "Next strike further
OTM once" made OI worse in **7 of 9** measured cases (RDDT $180->$182.50 = -99%,
OI 6; NVS -88%; NU -70%; GOOG -65%). Both improvements were steps onto ROUND
strikes ($530 +126%, NBIS $200C OI 4,570). Liquidity clusters at round and
psychological strikes, not by distance from spot — and OTM is also the direction
where premium falls and % spread mechanically worsens.

**Changes made (all five approved):**
1. **Strike band replaces the step-out.** Price `strike_search_steps_itm` (1)
   through `strike_search_steps_otm` (2) around the ATM anchor; among strikes
   passing every gate take the one CLOSEST TO ATM. Refinement worth recording:
   closest-to-ATM, not best-liquidity, so the band widens the search without
   changing the strike preference or drifting the delta profile.
2. **Depth gate is order-relative:** max(`min_quote_size_floor` 5, ceil(qty x
   `quote_size_coverage_multiple` 2)) on both sides. Tightens large orders (a
   16-lot now needs 32 a side, roughly the depth U $40C lacked) and relaxes only
   small ones. Sizing now runs BEFORE the depth check, since the requirement
   depends on quantity; on a depth failure, try the next-smaller quantity once.
3. **`data/chain_log.csv` added** — every strike priced, pass or fail, plus a
   cheap ATM probe for candidates whose tape did NOT qualify. Tape runs before
   contract selection, so a tape-blocked name was never re-priced: on 8/14 all
   four candidates were priced once at 9:38 and never again, leaving "spreads
   tighten later" untested for exactly the names it was invoked for. Backfilled
   with 41 readings from the journals. First correction it produced: counting
   distinct strike readings rather than prose mentions, spread (21) blocks
   marginally MORE often than OI (19) — the opposite of the earlier estimate.
4. **Gate-exception shadow track retired** (config keys removed, entry.md
   TEMPORARY section and the exit.md hook deleted). Its entire live output was
   one paper trade, SE, -$360; and it could never apply to quote-size, the gate
   that actually blocks. This closes the review that was due after today's close.
5. **Spread stays at 10, OI stays at 500.** Spread is the best-behaved gate
   (U $40C 30.8% -> 12.0% -> 7.7% across 12 minutes: correctly held out, then
   let in) and convergence is NOT reliable (SE oscillated 12-59% with no trend;
   CELH widened 16.4% -> 30.3%), so waiting it out is not a strategy. OI is the
   weakest of the three — NBIS $250C had OI 5,872 and one contract displayed a
   side; U $40C had OI 1,393 and swept 26% through its stop — but it is held
   because thin OI and wide spreads co-move, and the band search should clear
   most OI failures by finding the round strike without touching the threshold.

## Backtest — last month's data
Ran the leg rule against real bars. Harness `tools/backtest_legs.py`, bars in
`data/bars/`.

**What is testable and what is not.** `get_equity_historicals` gives 5-min OHLCV
back at least a month, so the leg rule is fully testable. `get_option_historicals`
returns **price bars only — no volume, no OI, no bid/ask, no depth**, and quotes
are real-time only. So historical OI, spread and displayed size do not exist
anywhere reachable: **the strike-band search and the depth gate cannot be
backtested at all.** Said plainly rather than approximated.

**Leg rule: 160 decision points, 4 names, 4 sessions.** Both rules share an
identical volume test, so they differ only on clock vs. structure.
| rule | fires | avg fwd-60min | wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| OLD (15-min clock) | 6 | -0.76% | 2/6 |
| NEW (volume + structure) | 4 | -0.21% | 2/4 |
They disagree on exactly 2 of 160 points, both BIRK 8/13, and structure declined
both (-0.79%, -2.91%). On NBIS 8/12 — the only real uptrend in the sample — the
two rules are **identical**: same 4 firings, first at 15:55 UTC, exactly the
breakout leg the journal identified, measured 2.5-2.9x vs the journal's 2.9x.

**This corrects what I told the user after the rule change.** I said BIRK would
have been taken under the revised rule and lost. It would not be. BIRK's 14:25
bar printed a new session high on 4.3x volume and closed $0.07 BELOW its own
open — a rejection candle. The clock cannot see that; structure fails it at the
bar instead of one bar later.

**The backtest found a real defect in the rule as written.** "Measure against the
quiet period immediately preceding the leg" is not a definition — it depends on
where the leg is judged to start. On BIRK the codified consecutive-closes start
walked the baseline into the busy post-open window and gave **0.48x** where the
journal's eyeballed dormant window gave **3.35x**: same leg, same bars, 7x apart,
committing the inflated-opening error the rule exists to prevent. Replaced with a
mechanical trailing median (`late_entry_baseline_bars: 6`,
`late_entry_baseline_skip_open_bars: 3`). Also made explicit the condition the
backtest showed is load-bearing: the decision bar must close in the trade
direction **relative to its own open**. Both were implicit before; the rule was
not reproducible for four weeks and running it was the only way to find out.

**Cross-sectional checks on a live chain (RDDT 9/18, 11 strikes):**
- Round-strike clustering **confirmed** — multiples of 10 average 2,164 OI vs
  1,087 for intermediate $5 strikes, **1.99x**, with no monotonic relation to
  distance from spot. Supports the band search premise.
- OI vs displayed depth: **r = -0.22**, slightly negative. The highest-OI strike
  on the board ($170, OI 3,307) had nearly the thinnest book (bid 15/ask 9).
  Confirms from data what had been argued from two anecdotes.

**Caveats.** 4 names; every session except NBIS was a no-trade day, so absolute
returns are unrepresentative — all 6 OLD signals and 3 of 4 NEW signals lost.
Outcome is underlying forward return; option P&L would be a levered,
theta-decayed version, same sign but larger. The chain test is one expiry on one
name.

## Backtest extended — 9 names, 360 decision points
Rebuilt the sample around the actual trade record, since the first pass had no
puts and only one winner: TSLA (+$920 put), GOOGL (+$405 put), PLTR (+$725),
NBIS (uptrend) vs. AAPL (-$1,568 put), U (-$1,536), BIRK, RDDT, NU.

**Outcome metric changed to max favourable/adverse excursion to session end.** A
fixed 60-min horizon misprices this strategy both ways — the ratchet lets winners
run, the -25% stop caps losers. TSLA proves it: made +$920 in reality, reads
**-1.85%** at 60 minutes.

| rule | fires | avg MFE | avg MAE | MAE <= -1.5% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OLD (clock) | 8 | +0.96% | -2.17% | 4/8 |
| NEW (structure) | 7 | +0.93% | -1.38% | 2/7 |

Same upside, materially less downside — NEW halves the deeply-adverse rate. All
three disagreements favour it: declines BIRK twice (-0.79%, -2.91%), adds one U
signal at +0.35%. It is not simply more conservative; it fires where OLD doesn't.

**The important finding is a false negative neither rule fixes.** Both miss two
of the four real winners, GOOGL and PLTR, and the cause is isolated to **the
volume gate alone**. PLTR: tape passed all 40 decision bars, structure never
blocked once, volume blocked every bar, peaking **1.36x** vs the 1.5 threshold.
GOOGL peaked **1.34x**. Both were steady grinds — and a steady grind never prints
a 1.5x bar.

This is a population-calibration error. 1.5 was derived from a record consisting
almost entirely of **gapped earnings-reaction names** (NBIS, BIRK, SMCI, RDDT)
where volume arrives in bursts. On an ordinary trending name it is near
unreachable. Sweep (`tools/backtest_legs.py --sweep`) shows 1.5 sitting in the
weakest part of the curve — MFE/MAE ratio bottoms at 1.4-1.5 (~0.6) and improves
both below (1.74 at 1.20, 4/4 winners) and above (2.72 at 2.00).

**Deliberately did NOT retune.** n=9, and the 1.4-1.5 trough is well inside noise
for that sample. Picking 1.20 off this curve is the same overfitting the repo
already refused once — 14 DTE was chosen over the measured 16-DTE peak because
"n=20 is too small to justify fitting the exact maximum." Recorded instead: the
value is unsupported, sits in a weak region, and its misses are systematic rather
than random. Revisit with live `leg_log.csv` data, and consider that the fix may
not be a different number but a volume test that doesn't assume burst-shaped
volume.

**Harness bug found and fixed while adding the sweep:** running the file as a
script makes it `__main__`, so `import backtest_legs as B; B.VOL_RATIO=th` bound
a second module object and `analyze()` never saw the change — every threshold
returned identical results. Caught because 9 rows of a sensitivity sweep were
byte-identical, which is not a thing that happens. Now sets its own module global.

## Threshold question: should 1.5 become 1.2? — No
User asked directly. Answer is no, and it required correcting the sweep I had
just presented.

**The MFE/MAE sweep measured the wrong thing.** Two errors: it counted every
qualifying bar as a signal when only the **first per name-day** becomes a trade;
and a signal with excellent MFE still loses if it trips the -25% stop on the way
there. Added `tools/backtest_legs.py --pnl`, which applies the real exit cascade
(hard TP 50, ratchet arm 12 / trail 20, early floor, stop -25) to an option
position at 7.9x leverage with 14-DTE theta and 3% round-trip friction, one trade
per name-day:

| threshold | trades | avg | total | wins |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.20 | 8 | -7.5% | **-60%** | 3/8 |
| 1.50 (live) | 5 | -7.8% | **-39%** | 1/5 |
| 1.75 | 3 | -3.5% | -11% | 1/3 |
| 2.00 | 2 | +0.4% | +1% | 1/2 |

**1.20 is worse than 1.50** — near-identical average but more losing trades, so
the total is half again as bad. Stable across leverage 5x-10x. Catching 4/4
winners bought less than it cost.

The gradient now points UP rather than down, but 2.00 rests on two trades, the
table is dominated by three no-trade days where any entry stops out (and where
the liquidity gates blocked the trade in reality before the leg rule mattered),
and the model contradicts reality on GOOGL — it shows a loss where the real trade
made +$405, because the real entry wasn't at the signal bar and was exited
discretionarily. Not validated either.

**Two defensible metrics disagreeing on n=9 is the result: this sample cannot
settle the threshold in either direction.** Left at 1.5, not because it's right
but because nothing here justifies moving it.

The durable finding is structural and no threshold fixes it: the test assumes
**burst-shaped volume**. Steady trends never print a 1.5x bar, and lowering the
line far enough to admit them admits everything else too (at 1.20, 4 of 5 losers
fire). The candidate fix is a differently-shaped test — sustained elevation over
a window, or volume against the name's own intraday distribution — not a
different constant.
