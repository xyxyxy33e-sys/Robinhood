## Diagnostic (9:32 ET) — stop_limit during the 9:30-9:45 blackout (NOT a strategy trade)
- **Purpose:** follow-up to 2026-07-31's discussion — does the 9:30-9:45 stop_market
  blackout (`OPTION_STOP_MARKET_INVALID_TIME_MARKET_OPEN`) apply to stop_limit
  orders too, or only to stop_market by name? If stop_limit is accepted, resting a
  stop_limit at entry could let entry time revert from 9:45 back to 9:35.
- **Contract tested:** AAPL $305C 8/07 (4 DTE), instrument
  `3ffd9b62-5880-4322-b38d-26c929325653`. Live quote at test time: bid $5.30 / ask
  $5.70 (OI 7,331, tight spread) — liquid, freshly quoted.
- **Test order:** stop_limit BUY-to-open, 1 contract, stop_price $10.25 (~80% above
  the $5.70 ask), limit_price $10.30 — deliberately unreachable, zero real fill risk.
  Placed at 9:32:50 ET (account 576391551, ref_id
  `76d34af9-4833-432f-acf6-686477880ca1`), order id `6a709882-60df-4a5a-b886-b6d0e8d8eea0`.
- **Result: ACCEPTED.** `place_option_order` returned immediately (no rejection);
  `get_option_orders` showed `state: confirmed` within 1 second — no
  `OPTION_STOP_MARKET_INVALID_TIME_MARKET_OPEN`-style error, no other time-based
  rejection. Cancelled immediately at 9:32:59 ET; `get_option_orders` confirmed
  `state: cancelled`, `processed_quantity: 0.00000` — no fill, clean cancel.
- **Finding:** stop_limit orders ARE accepted during the 9:30-9:45 blackout window,
  unlike stop_market. This confirms the reasoning from 7/31: the blackout is scoped
  specifically to stop_market (an unbounded-price order type), not to stops in
  general — a stop_limit becomes a bounded limit order once triggered, which is not
  the risk the 9:30-9:45 restriction targets.
- **Implication:** reverting entry time from 9:45 back to 9:35 with a resting
  stop_limit (instead of stop_market) during the blackout is now a technically
  viable option — worth a discussion on whether to make that change, since it was
  only ruled out on 7/31 pending this exact test. Not changed yet; this diagnostic
  is research-only per the user's instruction. Tradeoffs to weigh before deciding:
  a resting stop_limit can go unfilled on a fast gap-through (the limit leg has no
  guarantee, unlike stop_market's guaranteed-exit property), so reverting to 9:35
  would trade "assured but possibly slower-to-rest" protection for "resting sooner
  but not guaranteed to fill" protection — a real design tradeoff, not a strict
  improvement.
