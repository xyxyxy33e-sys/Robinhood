### An OI observation worth measuring, not asserting

PYPL gapped -13.6%, so its §2.2 band now sits at strikes 51-54 where 09-11 put OI is
**0 / 20 / 11 / 3**. The same expiry's OI peaks at **302 (strike 60)** and 144 (62) — i.e.
clustered around *yesterday's* $61.47 price, not today's $53.10. The 09-18 monthly at
strike 53 carries 60 against the weekly's 11.

The tempting conclusion is "large gaps displace the §2.2 band away from where OI sits, and
that is why gappers keep screening chain-dead." **I am not claiming that.** PYPL's chain
fails regardless — its peak of 302 is itself below the 500 gate, so displacement is not
what killed it here. Three mechanisms I proposed this week were later refuted, so this goes
down as a measurable question for the weekend: for each corpus name-day, compare band OI
against the expiry's peak OI and against the gap size. If displacement is real it will show
up as a correlation; if it is not, it will not. Added as weekend item 12.
