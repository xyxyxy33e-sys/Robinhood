## Strategy change: opening gap-fade guard (~11:20 ET, user-prompted)

User observation: "crwv and nbis are in a similar situation, after massive spike,
people start to selloff, take profit after market open, is there a way we can monitor
that to avoid what happened today." Investigated properly rather than assuming.

**What actually distinguished today's two losers from the winner.** All three cleared
every existing gate at entry. What differed was *where in the opening move* each was
bought:

| | open-bar close pos | gap vs prior close | session hi at entry | entry | below hi | vs VWAP | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CRWV | **0.21** | +20.1% | 111.82 | 110.99 | **−0.74%** | +1.75% | STOP −27.19% |
| NBIS | **0.27** | +17.7% | 231.95 | 230.00 | **−0.84%** | +2.62% | STOP −25.63% |
| SMCI | **0.83** | +10.7% | 37.17 | 35.82 | **−3.63%** | −0.66% | WIN +3.96% |

Two measures separate them, and both were available *before* each entry:

1. **Opening-bar close position** = (close − low) / (high − low) of the first 5-min
   RTH bar. CRWV closed in the bottom 21% of its opening range, NBIS the bottom 27% —
   both gaps were being *sold* by the first real regular-session size. SMCI closed in
   the top 83% — its gap was being *bought*.
2. **Distance below the session high at entry.** Both losers were bought within 0.85%
   of the session high — the top of the push. SMCI was bought 3.63% below its high,
   after the first pullback had completed.

**Out-of-sample check against 2026-08-11** (different day, different names, outcomes
already known and journaled — so this is a real test, not a re-fit):

| sym | day | side | open-bar close pos | verdict | what actually happened |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CRWV | 8/12 | call | 0.21 | REJECT | stopped out −27.2% |
| NBIS | 8/12 | call | 0.27 | REJECT | stopped out −25.6% |
| SMCI | 8/12 | call | 0.83 | ACCEPT | win +4.0% |

| LEGN | 8/11 | call | 0.24 | REJECT | qualified early, fully reversed by 10:25 |
| SE | 8/11 | call | 0.63 | ACCEPT | held conviction all session, strongest name |
| HIMS | 8/11 | put | 0.72 | REJECT | gap-down fully bought back within 15 min |

**7 of 7 classified correctly**, and the clusters are far apart — worst success 0.63
vs. best failure 0.27, so any threshold in 0.35-0.55 separates all seven. That width
matters: it means the result doesn't depend on a precisely-tuned number. There is also
a real mechanism behind it, not just a fitted statistic — the opening bar is the first
time regular-session size meets the premarket gap, and a close near that bar's low
means the size is distributing into the spike.

**Counterintuitive finding worth recording: VWAP was inverted here.** Both losers were
*above* session VWAP at entry (+1.75%, +2.62%) and the winner was *below* it (−0.66%).
A naive "must be above VWAP" entry gate would have green-lit both stop-outs and
blocked the one winner. Being extended above VWAP in the opening 15 minutes is a
warning, not a confirmation — the opposite of its meaning later in the session. Did
not add any VWAP-based entry gate as a result.

**Implemented** (config.yaml "Opening gap-fade guard" + entry.md §1.4), both
blocking-only — they can veto a candidate but never promote one, so the worst case is
a missed trade, never a worse one:
- `opening_fade_guard_enabled: true` — master switch.
- `opening_fade_guard_min_gap_pct: 5` — gates apply only to names that actually gapped
  (all 7 above gapped 6.6-20.1%); ordinary non-gap movers are exempt.
- **Gate A** `opening_bar_min_close_position: 0.50` — first 5-min bar must close in the
  upper half of its range for a call (lower half for a put). Cleared not by the clock
  but by a volume-confirmed 5-min close back above the opening bar's high. NBIS did
  exactly that today — reclaimed its $228.44 opening-bar high by 9:50 and ran to $234+
  by 10:05 — so a whole-day ban would have been wrong.
- **Gate B** `opening_window_chase_guard_pct: 1.5`, `opening_window_end_et: "10:30"` —
  before 10:30, reject any entry within 1.5% of the session high. 1.5% sits inside the
  empty band between the losers (≤0.84%) and the entries that worked (SMCI 3.63%, SE
  8/11 ~2.05% at its first near-miss).

Gate B alone would have blocked both of today's stop-outs and still permitted the SMCI
winner. Gate A additionally reads the name's character for the day (it flags the
persistent faders — RIOT, LEGN, CRWV — while passing the names that trended, SE and
SMCI).

**Caveat recorded deliberately:** n=7 across two days. Directionally strong and
mechanically motivated, but not proven. These gates only ever *cost* trades, which is
the right risk asymmetry for a change made mid-session on this much evidence — but
their live veto rate should be reviewed against outcomes before either is tightened.
Every veto is journaled with its measured numbers so that review is possible.

### ARITHMETIC CORRECTION — I misstated the protected floor
Throughout the ratchet phase I reported the worst case as **"+$132.00 if it triggers
and fills at the $1.26 limit"**, and even wrote the formula `11 × ($1.26 − $1.21) × 100`
next to it. That formula evaluates to **$55**, not $132. The $132 figure is
`11 × ($1.33 − $1.21) × 100` — i.e. the value at the **stop trigger price**, not at the
**limit fill price**. I computed the floor off the trigger while labelling it as the
limit-fill case.

**This mattered.** The figure appeared in the 14:28 decision prompt ("floor locked at
+$132") on which the user chose to HOLD rather than take ~+$242. The true protected
floor was **+$55**, so the actual downside of holding was giving back ~$187, not ~$110
— roughly **70% more risk than I represented**. The recommendation to hold may still
have been reasonable, but it was argued on a number that overstated the safety net, and
the user was entitled to the correct one.

Root cause: a stop_limit has *two* prices and I conflated them. For any future
stop_limit, the guaranteed floor is the **limit** price (and even that is not
guaranteed — a gap through it can leave the order unfilled entirely).
