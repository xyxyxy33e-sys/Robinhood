# Review and follow-up tests (2026-09-29)

All numbers monthly-rebalanced, equal-weight, current-S&P-500 universe (survivorship bias remains the largest unresolved issue), no costs unless stated.

## 1. Fixes to the live setup
- **Stale signals (bug):** the monthly rebalance built its target list from a price cache last updated 2026-09-22, so the Oct 1 rebalance would have traded on old data. Added `data_refresh.py` (incremental refresh, full rebuild if the cache is missing) and a repo-local `.cache/` (gitignored). `barbell_30.py` now refreshes first and restricts to current index members.
- **Routine timing:** the rebalance job ran at 15:00 UTC (11am ET, mid-session, not "at close" as I earlier said). It now runs 21:30 UTC (after the close) on days 1-3, and `monthly_run.py` itself exits unless today is the first NYSE trading day. A weekly Friday mark-to-market job was added.
- **Data checks:** SNDK's +1,687% is real (listed Feb 2025 at $36; no split-like jump). MRNA shows a +177% single-day move on 2026-08-19 that I have not verified. A routine run on 2026-09-25 (not a scheduled day) left no trace in state/ledger.

## 2. Correction: barbell drawdown history
Early backtests silently *skipped* months when fewer than 20 zero-yield names had positive momentum (9 months, Oct 2008 - May 2009, plus Jun 2022), which equals sitting in cash through the crash. The live paper trail instead stayed fully invested in fewer names. Explicit comparison, 2006-10 onward:

| Treatment | CAGR | Sharpe | Max DD | 2008 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Skip (all cash when a leg is short) | 26.6% | 1.31 | -33.0% | -30.4% |
| Hold what qualifies + cash (SHY) in empty slots | 26.8% | 1.30 | -42.3% | -34.5% |
| Hold what qualifies, fully invested in fewer names | 26.6% | 1.27 | -46.9% | -36.3% |

From 2010 on the three are identical. So the earlier "20-year max drawdown improved from -49.7% to -32.1%" claim was overstated; a realistic figure is about -42% to -47%. The paper trail now fills unfilled growth slots with SHY (matches the cash-slot treatment).

## 3. Walk-forward validation (`wf_validation.py`)
Design chosen on a train window, judged on an unseen test window (2019-01 to 2026-08).
- **Barbell** (96 configs: leg sizes, screens, absolute momentum, lookback; train 2006-10..2018-12): every config beat SPY in the test window (SPY Sharpe 1.07; barbell configs mean 1.45). Rank correlation train->test is only 0.38. Our default (10 HY + 20 growth) ranked 58th/96 in train and 35th/96 in test, i.e. it is not a cherry-picked winner; the edge is broad across designs. Smaller books (5 HY + 10 growth) scored best in both windows. Caveat: the train window contains 2008 and the test window is a strong momentum bull, so the higher test Sharpe partly reflects the regime.
- **Buyback** (64 configs; train 2010-05..2018-12): mean Sharpe 1.22 in train and 1.22 in test (no degradation); 89% of configs beat SPY in test (1.07); default (30, blend, momentum filter) ranked 5th in train and 19th in test (Sharpe 1.26). The best-in-train config (10 names, blend, momentum) was also #1 in test (1.49).

*35% short-term / 20% long-term on realized gains, netted per calendar year, paid the next January; dividends not separately taxed (flatters high-yield names).

The filter roughly halves the barbell's drawdown for ~2 points of CAGR, but the benefit comes almost entirely from one event (2008), and both filters made 2022 worse (whipsaw). It does not help the buyback book.

## 6. Sector cap on the growth leg (`sector_cap.py`)
Capping the growth leg at 3-8 names per sector changes little: Sharpe 1.27-1.30 versus 1.30 uncapped, max drawdown -42% to -44% either way, CAGR falls from 26.8% to 24.6% at a cap of 3. Today's growth leg is 12/20 Information Technology; a cap of 4 would spread it across 7 sectors. Earlier apparent gains from capping came from the month-skipping artifact in section 2 and disappear once cash slots are explicit. No evidence the cap improves risk in this sample; it is a concentration-risk judgment call.
