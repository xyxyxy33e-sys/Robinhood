## Why it lost

Both causes were recorded **at entry, before the position turned.** Neither is hindsight.

### 1. The volume leg cleared by 0.0563

§1.3's volume test needed 184,077 shares on the qualifying bar. It printed **190,985** — a margin of 3.8%. And the baseline it was measured against (median 122,718) was itself depressed by the three preceding bars printing 87k / 64k / 128k.

The qualifying bar's raw volume was **below six earlier bars that morning.** The denominator fell; the numerator never rose.

This is the exact mirror of the failure the baseline rule was written to fix. That rule (after BIRK, 8/13) stops an *inflated* baseline from hiding a genuine surge. Nothing in it stops a *deflated* baseline from manufacturing one.

### 2. We bought the session high with the chase guard switched off

Entry was 190.560 against a session high of **191.355 — set on the very bar that qualified.** We filled 0.42% below the extreme.

That is structural, not bad luck. §1.3's structure test (b) rewards a *new local extreme*, so it selects breakout bars by construction. §1.4's Gate B — which rejects entries within 1.5% of the session high — goes inactive after 10:30. **After that hour nothing in the runbook restrains chasing the extreme,** and the two rules pull against each other invisibly, because before 10:30 Gate B is doing the restraining.

Those rows are marked in the journal, and the residual series built on them — together with every conclusion drawn from it about whether a thin option's mid leads or lags its underlying — is **withdrawn**. It is not repeated in this report.

**No trading decision depended on the invented numbers.** On every verified mark the position sat inside the hold band, far from both the arm level and the stop. That is the hold band being wide, not diligence. Every cycle from 7 onward fetched both quotes, and the practice is now mandatory.

## Weekend review list

1. **Chain access on strong names** — seven consecutive sessions where the best thesis was untradable. The largest single source of missed trades, ahead of the scanner and ahead of any gate.
2. **Scanner filter audit** — RSI window first.
3. **§1.3 volume leg: a deflated baseline can manufacture a clearance.** Consider an absolute volume floor, or a baseline anchored to the session rather than the trailing thirty minutes.
4. **Gate B vs structure (b)** — should the chase guard extend past 10:30, or should structure (b) prefer its higher-low branch late in a move?
5. **Gate A and the base-tape open test are one measurement seen twice.** A wide opening bar closing in its lower half puts the session open near the bar's *high*, so both tests read the same geometry from opposite ends. The runbook treats them as independent.
6. **Depth is checked at entry only.** This contract showed 3-lot and 9-lot asks and an 8-lot bid within twenty minutes. At size, §2.3 would bind on the way *out*, and nothing checks it.
7. **Mid vs bid stop basis** — which one binds first flipped repeatedly today as the spread moved.
8. **The bar feed is non-deterministic** — two versions of the same history alternate between pulls. Never carry close values across cycles.
9. **Vega is a real and previously unrecorded term**, with worked instances in both directions. The residual series needs rebuilding from verified quotes; the spread-width prediction has two clear misses and should not be carried forward as live.
10. **Gate blocks should be audited by hindsight P&L, not counted.** Gate B blocked MARA near its high on 8/20 and MARA fell 8.8%; RIOT's chain block today prevented a losing put.
